## Choice and trade-off

One global processing run starts each Monday at 10:00 am Australia/Sydney, observing daylight
saving. Its new batch groups eligible earnings per barber. Only earnings available strictly
before the scheduled instant qualify; equality waits until the next week. A late worker retains
the scheduled cut-off, not its actual start time. Existing release, refund and dispute gates remain.
Every positive eligible AUD total qualifies, including one cent; there is no additional QuickTrimr
minimum or small-positive-balance carry threshold. Zero never produces a payment.

Weekly processing limits payout frequency and operational work. The cost is that earnings missing
the cut-off wait nearly a week before processing, with settlement and bank delays on top. Option B
would have processed daily at the same local time, reducing this wait but increasing payout
frequency. Neither option guaranteed a particular bank-arrival day.

QuickTrimr bears standard Connect and bank-payout fees without reducing barber entitlement or
adding a client surcharge. Actual provider costs are reconciled at their billed scope, separately
from booking snapshots. This is an additional approved decision, not an assumption that P0-D02's
payment-processing fee policy already covered Connect. Historical commission/cancellation examples
still exclude these costs and remain valid; they do not claim to calculate profit.

## Lifecycle and failure interpretation

- The run starts processing, not guaranteed bank credit. Funding a connected Stripe account and
  paying its bank are separate operations with separate references. Only the mapped bank payout's
  confirmed paid state produces `paid_out`; a whole batch is paid only if all its items succeeded.
- A confirmed temporary failure waits for the next weekly run. Invalid bank details or account
  restrictions require a visible hold, barber/admin notification and verified correction before
  the next-run retry. A lack of settled funds never becomes a platform advance or barber deduction.
- Unknown outcomes reconcile first. Persisted provider identities and operation/attempt records
  are needed in addition to deterministic server-derived keys. A retry resumes the original
  obligation, does not allocate its earnings again, and never repeats a successful funding transfer.
  A replacement bank payout requires confirmed failure/cancellation and reconciled returned funds.
- A late bank failure corrects the earning/item/batch projections and adds audit evidence without
  deleting history. It is not automatic permission to pay again before reconciliation.
- Holds/uncertainty surface immediately to operations with age, deadline and next action. Provider
  holding limits must be respected; indefinite silent carry, confiscation, forced refunds or sending
  funds to an unverified destination are not approved remedies. Escalate with Stripe before its limit.
  This includes otherwise available balances blocked before a batch item exists: the lack of an
  attempted transfer must not hide a bank/account hold from either its owner or operations.
- Barber copy shows the next processing date/timezone, status and an estimated arrival when known.
  An unknown estimate stays unknown; a blocked item does not promise payment at the next run.
  No instant payouts, barber withdrawal button or admin manual-transfer button is introduced.

## Provider evidence checked on 2026-10-02

- [Stripe connected-account payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/payouts-connected-accounts)
  describes configuration-dependent platform controls and account-scoped payout events. Account
  type/control support must be demonstrated in P0-T18 and applied by P1-T07, not guessed here.
- [Stripe manual payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manual-payouts) distinguishes internal
  transfers from bank payouts, limits payouts to available funds, and currently lists a 90-day
  holding limit applicable to Australia. This is a provider constraint, not a new QuickTrimr timer
  or permission to hold funds for that long routinely.
- [Stripe payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts) documents Australian minimums, bank delays and
  the possibility of a later failure after an initial paid status. Provisioning must verify the
  actual account's constraints. The chosen no-extra-minimum policy is a business choice.
- [Stripe idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) explains key
  retention can expire after at least 24 hours. A weekly retry therefore needs durable local
  reconciliation and cannot rely on replaying the same key alone.
- [Australian Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/au/connect/pricing) separates account, payout
  and other applicable platform costs. Per-payout charges inform the batching trade-off, but no
  quoted tariff is frozen into business logic; the actual arrangement and invoice govern.

These sources constrain implementation. They do not prove our tenant supports the policy, that
funds will arrive on a particular date, or that a production integration passes its tests.
